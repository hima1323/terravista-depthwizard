# DepthWizard updates, 15 October 2026

Five new disaster-response tools in the web viewer, plus a small backend addition they need.
All run in the browser on data the app already produces: no new Python packages and no extra model runs.

Changed files: `SynRS3D/web/index.html` (viewer) and `SynRS3D/app.py` (backend).

---

## 1. Connected flooding ("Water from")

**Before:** the Flood tool used a bathtub model. Every point below the water level flooded, even low spots
cut off behind higher ground or embankments that water cannot actually reach.

**Now:** a new **Water from** selector next to the water-level slider:

| Option | What floods |
|---|---|
| **all low ground** | Everything below the water level (the old behaviour, unchanged) |
| **scene edges** | Water enters from outside the image (river, sea) and floods only ground it can flow to |
| **points I click** | Water spreads from points you place (a river bank, canal, breached embankment). Use the "＋ Place water sources" / "Clear sources" buttons in the flood card |

- **How it works:** a priority-flood over the terrain grid. Each cell gets the lowest water level at which
  water from the source can reach it. The connection is 4-way, so water does not leak diagonally through a
  one-cell embankment. The flood card counts (area, blocks submerged / partly flooded, floor area) only
  include buildings the water can reach.
- **3D:** the water surface is now drawn only where water reaches, instead of one flat plane over the whole
  scene. The GLB export and the street-level camera ("float on flooded streets") use the same water.
- **"When does this point flood?"** Hovering in 2D or clicking in 3D now shows either "under X m of water"
  or "floods when the water reaches +Y m (Z m above now)" for any point.

## 2. Route finder between two points (⤳ Route)

- Click **⤳ Route**, then click the map (2D or 3D) to place **A** and **B**.
- The route (A* search on the 512 × 512 grid) avoids:
  - buildings and water bodies (from the model's land-cover output),
  - steep slopes,
  - **water deeper than the limit** at the current flood level.
- It prefers roads. Paths can't squeeze diagonally between two blocked cells.
- **Travel modes:**

  | Mode | Water depth limit | Slope limit | Prefers roads |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | On foot | 0.5 m | 30° | mildly |
  | Car | 0.3 m | 15° | strongly |

  The depth limit can be edited.
- **The card shows:**
  - length and estimated time (slower through water),
  - metres of route through water and the deepest water crossed,
  - the share of the route on roads.
- On the map, dry stretches are green and stretches through water are blue.
- **If there's no route,** it says why: either the water is too deep (a dry-weather route exists) or
  buildings, water bodies or slopes block every way.
- **▶ Fly** flies along the route at street level in 3D, using the existing fly-through.
- The route recalculates automatically when the water level changes.

## 3. Nearest safe place

**Nearest safe place** finds the closest spot reachable from A that is one of:

- high ground at least **1 m above** the current water, or
- (optional checkbox) next to a **building with at least one full floor above the water**, for vertical
  evacuation.

The card says which kind of place it found (★ on the map), how far away it is, how long it takes to get
there, and how many of that building's floors are above water.

## 4. Rain & rise timeline (⏱ Timeline, in Flood mode)

Answers: *"from the current flood level, if the rain continues at this rate, what happens over the next days?"*

**Inputs:**
- rain in mm/h for N hours, or **Use rain forecast**: the free hourly forecast from Open-Meteo for the
  scene's location (GeoTIFF scenes; no API key; up to 16 days)
- river / outside water rise in m/day (e.g. a gauge trend or a CWC forecast)
- drain capacity in mm/h (default 25; Indian city drains are typically designed for 25–50 mm/h)
- area draining into the scene, as a multiple of the scene's own area (rain falling upstream that flows here)
- number of days to simulate

**Outputs:**
- **Time slider:** drag it to see the flood at any hour in 2D and 3D. All the existing flood numbers update.
- **Chart:** hourly rain bars plus the water level line.
- **Table:** water level, flooded area and number of blocks with water inside, at the end of each day.
- **Summary lines:**
  - total rain;
  - the wettest 24 h, with its **IMD category** (heavy / very heavy / extremely heavy …);
  - the peak water level and when it happens.
- **"Time until flooded" map:**

  | Colour | When it floods |
  |---|---|
  | purple | already under water |
  | red | within 6 h |
  | orange | 6–12 h |
  | yellow | 12–24 h |
  | light blue | day 2 |
  | blue | later |

  Buildings are coloured by when water first enters their ground floor.
- **Per building:** hovering shows "water reaches the ground floor in ≈ X h (day, time)".
- **Per point:** hovering shows when that spot goes under.
- **Route deadline:** with a route A → B set, it reports *"Route A → B stays open until ≈ Thu 14:00 (+31 h)"*,
  or that it's already blocked, or that it stays open the whole period.

**Model (kept simple on purpose, and stated in the app):**
- **Water level:** one water level for the flooded area, stepped hourly.
- **River water:** outside water rises at the rate you enter.
- **Rain water:** each hour, rain × runoff coefficient × the area draining here, minus what the drains carry
  away. The runoff coefficient is averaged from the model's land cover: roofs and roads 0.9, developed 0.8,
  bare ground 0.5, grassland 0.35, fields 0.3, trees 0.2.
- **Spreading:** that water fills the lowest ground the water can reach first (using the chosen "Water from"
  mode).
- **After the rain stops,** drains with spare capacity empty the rain water again.
- **It's a scenario tool, not a forecast.** It doesn't model:
  - how fast water flows,
  - blocked drains,
  - soil getting waterlogged,
  - water arriving from outside the image, except the river rise you enter.

## 5. Helipad & relief camp finder (🚁 Relief sites)

Click **🚁 Relief sites** in the bottom bar, or add `#sites` to the address. It lists up to 5 helicopter landing
sites and 5 relief camp sites. They're drawn on the 2D map (yellow dashed circles **H1–H5**, green areas
**C1–C5**) and in 3D (yellow rings, green markers).

**A spot counts as open ground when all of these hold:**
- no building;
- the model's land cover is not trees, water or buildings;
- nothing on it is 1 m or taller (cars, walls, shrubs);
- the terrain slope is at most the limit (default 5°);
- it is dry by the margin you set (default 0.5 m) above the current flood water. If the timeline is open, the
  default is to stay dry through its *highest* water level (checkbox).

**Helicopter landing sites**
- The largest clear circles of open ground. The default is a 25 m circle; the tooltip gives the rule of thumb
  (ICAO): 1.5 × the helicopter's length with rotors, about 24 m for an ALH Dhruv and about 38 m for a Mi-17.
  Confirm with the air wing.
- Clearance is measured with an exact distance transform, so it works however the streets are oriented
  (Chandigarh's grid is diagonal).
- Sites are picked most-clear first, at least one circle-width apart.
- **Each site shows:** clear diameter, slope, metres above water, and the tallest obstacle on the approach
  within 30 m of the circle.

**Relief camp sites**
- The largest connected open areas, counting only ground at least 3 m from any obstacle (so no slivers between
  buildings). The default minimum is 1,000 m².
- **Each site shows:** area, capacity at **45 m² per person** (Sphere humanitarian standard; can be edited),
  distance to the nearest dry road, slope, and metres above water.

**Linked to the other tools:**
- **Route here** makes the site the destination of the route finder: set A, and you get the walking or driving
  route to the helipad or camp.
- Results update automatically when the water level, flood source or timeline changes.

**Tested:**
- **Dry, Chandigarh scene:** 5 helipad sites (best: 53 m clear circle, 0.2° slope) and 5 camp sites (largest
  7,107 m² ≈ 157 people, 11 m from a road).
- **At the timeline's peak water (+2.55 m):** 2 helipad sites and 2 camp sites remain.
- **Self-test checks pass:** every cell inside each helipad circle is open, circles don't overlap, and camp
  capacity = area ÷ 45.

**Limits:**
- Slope comes from the 30 m terrain data, so a small ditch or kerb won't show.
- Overhead wires and poles aren't detected.
- Always inspect a site, and the helicopter approach path, before using it.

## Backend (app.py)

New `ensure_extras()` step, run after each scene is processed. It also fills in scenes that were already
processed when the server starts, without re-running the model:
- writes `<scene>_lc.f32`, the land-cover map at the browser grid size (used for route costs and rain runoff);
- adds `center_lonlat` to the scene's `.json` metadata (used for the rain forecast); this is `null` for
  images without georeferencing.

**Restart `app.py` once** to pick this up. The viewer page itself is re-read on every request.

## Testing

- `#selftest-toolstest` (in the address bar) runs automatic checks and writes the results into the page title:
  - the flood level never comes out below ground level;
  - routes never cross buildings and are never shorter than the straight-line distance;
  - the water-volume ↔ water-level conversion round-trips correctly;
  - over the timeline, the peak-so-far water level never decreases.
- **Results on the Chandigarh Sector 17 scene:**
  - All four checks passed.
  - A 702 m route was found where the straight-line distance is 595 m.
  - 60 mm/h of rain for 6 h, with the area draining here set to ×5, gave a peak of +2.55 m.
  - 14 of 28 blocks got water inside.
  - At that peak, the A → B route is correctly reported as blocked for people on foot.
- `#selftest-toolstest-toolsview` also leaves the route and the "time until flooded" map on screen, for
  screenshots.
- **Already failing before these changes:** the existing `#selftest` reports `ok=false` on this scene. Its
  largest block is ring-shaped, so the block's centre point is in a courtyard and the "building at centroid"
  check fails. The new tools don't affect this.

## Known limits

- **Terrain:** the ground model is 30 m Copernicus data (about ±2–4 m). That uncertainty is about ten times
  the 0.3–0.5 m depth limits used for routes, so treat route depths and "time until flooded" as indicative.
- **Roads:** these come from the model's land-cover output, which can miss roads hidden under trees.
- **Scene size:** a scene is about 525 m across, so routes and safe places are neighbourhood-scale and can't
  leave the image.
- **No terrain:** for PNG/JPG inputs (no georeferencing), the ground is flat, so connected flooding has
  nothing to work with and all low ground floods at once.
